The Parisi formula
in Lean

Following Talagrand’s proof, one checked argument at a time.

Qiang Wu · Updated 5 September 2026

I am working on a Lean 4 formalization of Talagrand’s proof of the Parisi formula. The project follows his 2006 Annals of Mathematics paper, in the Sherrington–Kirkpatrick (SK) model with exact covariance.

The formula identifies the limiting free entropy of the SK model with the Parisi variational value. The aim here is to formalize the published proof, reusing Mathlib and existing spin-glass libraries wherever possible.

The full proof is not yet complete. Guerra’s upper bound is proved. Talagrand’s Theorem 2.2 remains open.

What the Lean statement says

For inverse temperature β > 0 and external field h, the target is convergence of the expected log partition function per spin, sN = E[log ZN]/N, to the Parisi value as N tends to infinity. This is a theorem about a Gaussian spin system, not a numerical approximation to the variational problem.

A finite replica-symmetry-breaking scheme is represented by RSBScheme: overlap levels and masses, together with their ordering and endpoint conditions. The Gaussian recursion defines a functional for each scheme; parisiValue is its infimum over all schemes and all finite depths. The formalization already proves that this infimum is over a nonempty, bounded-below set, and that a minimizer exists at each fixed depth.

Where the proof stands

The checked Guerra identity expresses the interpolation derivative as an explicit correction minus a nonnegative, mass-weighted squared-overlap remainder. Integrating this identity gives the upper bound. The proof includes Gaussian integration by parts, differentiation through the finite cascade and its outer expectation, and continuity at the interpolation endpoints.

The final declaration, parisi_formula, already deduces convergence from this upper bound and Theorem 2.2. It still depends on the unfinished theorem: having that last deduction written in Lean does not make the full result complete.

The route to Theorem 2.2

Talagrand’s lower-bound argument studies two replicas—two spin configurations sampled from the same random system—and controls their overlap. For schemes that minimize at their own depth and are sufficiently close to the Parisi value, Theorem 2.2 asks for convergence of the interpolated pressure on every interval 0 ≤ t ≤ t0 < 1.

The concentration-to-convergence argument is checked: an explicit overlap-concentration hypothesis controls the interpolation remainder, and a differential inequality forces the gap to vanish. The missing input is the uniform two-replica estimate of Theorem 2.4, obtained through the interpolation and optimality arguments in Sections 3–5.

To finish that estimate, we still need to assemble the full replica-covariance derivative inequality, integrate it with endpoint control, establish the remaining uniform second-mass and optimality bounds, and cover the outstanding overlap and sign cases. The roadmap records the checked inputs and the precise remaining obligations.

Recent work inside the formalization

Replica derivatives. The Hessian of the actual nested two-replica recursion is now expressed through its normalized replica weights. Its averaged SK covariance contraction and the individual variance-level heat identities are also checked. These identify the pieces of the second interpolation; their combined inequality is still to be proved.

Scalar curvature. The negative-square identity behind Talagrand’s U″ has been propagated through the full outer recursion. This proves −1 ≤ U″ ≤ 0 at interior variance, with the baseline-mass restriction below one required by the current U′ identity. It does not assert second derivatives at the variance endpoints.

Uniform mass estimates. First and second mass derivatives of the scalar Gaussian transform are bounded uniformly, including mass zero. The first-derivative bound also extends through the full nested recursion with a constant independent of depth. The corresponding full nested second-derivative bound remains open.

What formalization adds

Much of the work is in the analytic details: justifying differentiation under an expectation, checking integrability, and treating zero masses without dividing by zero. Mathlib supplies general analysis and probability tools; the pinned RSAT dependency supplies reusable spin-glass and finite-recursion results. Local proofs connect these results to Talagrand’s definitions, checking their hypotheses, signs and normalization.

The repository separates the supporting library from the main proof targets. Its full check script builds both and runs axiom regression checks on completed results. These reject dependencies on Lean’s unfinished-proof placeholder, sorryAx, or additional axioms beyond the standard Lean foundations. Open targets remain explicitly marked: a successful build alone is not a certificate for the full Parisi formula.
