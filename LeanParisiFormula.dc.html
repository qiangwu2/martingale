The Parisi formula
in Lean

Following Talagrand’s proof, one checked argument at a time.

Qiang Wu · Updated 5 September 2026

I am working on a Lean 4 formalization of Talagrand’s proof of the Parisi formula. The project follows his 2006 Annals of Mathematics paper, in the Sherrington–Kirkpatrick (SK) model with exact covariance.

The Parisi formula characterizes the limit of the free energy of the SK model through a variational principle. The aim here is to formalize the published proof, reusing Mathlib and existing spin-glass libraries wherever possible.

The full proof is not yet complete. Guerra’s upper bound is proved. Talagrand’s Theorem 2.2 remains open.

What the Lean statement says

For inverse temperature β > 0 and external field h, the target is convergence of the free energy, FN = E[log ZN]/N, to the value given by the Parisi formula as N tends to infinity. This is a theorem about a Gaussian spin system, not a numerical approximation to the variational problem.

A finite replica-symmetry-breaking scheme consists of overlap levels and masses satisfying ordering and endpoint conditions. The Gaussian recursion defines the Parisi functional for each scheme; the Parisi formula takes its infimum over all schemes and all finite numbers of levels. The formalization already proves that this infimum is over a nonempty, bounded-below set, and that a minimizer exists for each fixed number of levels.

Where the proof stands

The checked Guerra identity expresses the interpolation derivative as an explicit correction minus a nonnegative, mass-weighted squared-overlap remainder. Integrating this identity gives the upper bound. The proof includes Gaussian integration by parts, differentiation through the finite cascade and its outer expectation, and continuity at the interpolation endpoints.

The final deduction of the Parisi formula from this upper bound and Theorem 2.2 is already written in Lean. It still depends on the unfinished theorem: having that last deduction written does not make the full result complete.

The route to Theorem 2.2

Talagrand’s lower-bound argument studies two replicas—two spin configurations sampled from the same random system—and controls their overlap. For schemes that minimize the Parisi functional at their own number of levels and whose functional values are sufficiently close to the infimum over all finite schemes, Theorem 2.2 asserts convergence of the interpolated free energy on every interval 0 ≤ t ≤ t0 < 1.

The concentration-to-convergence argument is checked: an explicit overlap-concentration hypothesis controls the interpolation remainder, and a differential inequality forces the gap to vanish. The missing input is the uniform two-replica estimate of Theorem 2.4, obtained through the interpolation and optimality arguments in Sections 3–5.

To finish that estimate, we still need to combine the replica derivative formulas to prove the interpolation inequality, integrate it with endpoint control, establish uniform bounds on second derivatives with respect to the mass parameters and the remaining optimality estimates, and cover the outstanding overlap and sign cases. The roadmap records the proved results and the precise remaining steps.

Recent work inside the formalization

Replica derivatives. The Hessian of the two-replica recursion is now expressed through its normalized replica weights. The contribution from the SK covariance after averaging over the disorder, and the derivative formulas for each Gaussian variance parameter, are also checked. These identify the terms in the second interpolation; the inequality obtained by combining them is still to be proved.

Curvature of Talagrand’s auxiliary function. The identity expressing U″ as a negative average of a square has been proved through all successive Gaussian averages. This gives −1 ≤ U″ ≤ 0 in the interior of the variance interval, when the relevant mass parameter is less than one, as required by the current proof of the U′ identity. It does not assert second derivatives at the variance endpoints.

Derivatives with respect to mass parameters. The first and second derivatives of the scalar Gaussian transform with respect to its mass parameter are bounded uniformly, including when that parameter is zero. The first-derivative bound also extends through the full recursion with a constant independent of the number of levels. The corresponding second-derivative bound for the full recursion remains open.

What formalization adds

Much of the work is in the analytic details: justifying differentiation under an expectation, checking integrability, and treating zero masses without dividing by zero. Mathlib supplies general analysis and probability tools; the RSAT library supplies reusable spin-glass and finite-recursion results. Local proofs connect these results to Talagrand’s definitions, checking their hypotheses, signs and normalization.

The repository separates supporting lemmas from the main theorems. Its verification script checks both and runs checks of proof dependencies on completed results. These reject dependence on unfinished proof placeholders or axioms beyond the standard Lean foundations. Unfinished results remain explicitly marked: a successful build alone is not a certificate for the full Parisi formula.
