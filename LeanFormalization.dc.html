Formalizing the Parisi Formula in Lean

A computer-checked development of Talagrand's proof for the Sherrington-Kirkpatrick spin glass model.

Project update · September 2026

Spin glasses turn simple random interactions into a difficult global geometry. The Parisi formula identifies the limiting free entropy of the SK model through a variational principle. This project is translating Talagrand's proof into Lean 4, where every deduction is checked by the proof assistant.

The Parisi formula is one of the central results in the mathematical theory of spin glasses. Michel Talagrand's proof combines ideas from probability, analysis, and statistical physics, including Gaussian integration by parts, recursive transforms, interpolation, and control of replica overlaps.

Why formalize this proof?

Proofs in spin glass theory often compress substantial analytic detail. A differentiation under an expectation may rely on measurability, integrability, domination, continuity, and endpoint identities that are natural to a specialist but remain implicit on paper. Lean requires each of these dependencies to be stated and verified.

That extra precision serves two purposes. It gives an independently checkable proof artifact, and it exposes the reusable mathematical infrastructure needed to formalize more results in probability and statistical physics. A formal proof does not replace a readable mathematical explanation; it complements one by making the logical chain mechanically auditable.

What is checked now

The development follows Talagrand's 2006 proof in the SK model with exact covariance. Completed results are protected by build-time axiom checks, while unfinished targets remain explicit in the repository.

The current frontier

The full Parisi formula is formalized modulo Theorem 2.2. The remaining work centers on the interpolation and optimality estimates that establish coupled-replica overlap concentration. The later convergence deduction from that concentration input is already represented in Lean.

This boundary is deliberately visible: a successful build checks the completed results, but it is not presented as certification of the unfinished theorem. The repository's roadmap records which statements are axiom-clean, which deductions are conditional, and where proof placeholders remain.

Reading the development

The main proof route begins in Targets/Talagrand.lean. The Guerra audit checks dependencies of the completed results, while the roadmap and blueprint connect the Lean files to the mathematical proof.

The project uses Lean 4 and Mathlib, together with existing formalized spin glass infrastructure. Source credits and pinned dependencies are documented in the repository.
